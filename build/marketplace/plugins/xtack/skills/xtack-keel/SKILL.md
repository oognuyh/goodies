# Xtack Keel

Keep searches and reads within the user's permitted roots; when access is limited to the project, do not search its parent or sibling directories.

For an independent ADR stakeholder, start a fresh context without the coordinator's prior deliberation (for example, `fork_turns: none` or `fork_context: false` when supported by the current Codex tool). Supply the actual outcome, decision, alternatives, constraints, and relevant evidence explicitly. If that separation cannot be established, do not claim independent stakeholder analysis.

Shape a technical design that meets the outcome and can be tested and changed. Separate technical design from unresolved product intent: investigate facts and delegated choices yourself; ask for essential intent only when it cannot reasonably be inferred. In unattended work, continue independent work and report a genuine blocker instead of interrupting the user. Do not make architecture review a prerequisite for a routine, well-understood change.

1. Establish the goal, success criteria, constraints, and existing contracts. Trace the affected entry points, callers, data and state, operational environment, and existing decisions before proposing changes. Distinguish requested requirements from stronger design choices, and current supported behavior and evidence from assumptions; identify the decisions that actually affect the outcome.
2. Define the important boundaries: responsibilities, interfaces, ownership, state transitions and invariants, affected consumers, failure behavior, and compatibility or migration where relevant. Start with representative caller use and sketch signatures, data shapes, or module boundaries when these clarify the design. Compare supported inputs with the design's mappings and environment limits; treat required call ordering and observable errors as part of the interface. Ask what callers must know and what changes together; prefer cohesive modules with useful boundaries rather than indirection that moves complexity to callers. When an abstraction is questionable, consider whether removing it would spread its complexity to callers.
3. Where a consequential choice is genuinely uncertain, compare viable, meaningfully different approaches, including the existing approach when applicable. Evaluate them against the actual constraints, failure modes, operational cost, reversibility, and verification. Use focused experiments or isolated prototypes to resolve questions that source inspection cannot; do not invent alternatives or claim a prototype proves production behavior. Independent design perspectives are useful when they can change the choice, not a mandatory panel for routine design.
4. Recommend a design with its reasoning, implications, unresolved risks, and concrete checks that could falsify its important guarantees. For implementation, identify a feasible sequence and integration boundaries when needed. When implementation exposes design friction or invalid assumptions, revisit the affected choice and its decision record rather than forcing the original shape; preserve superseded decisions. Preserve a design-only or plan-only stopping point; a broader task that explicitly authorizes implementation may continue.

When deciding an ADR-worthy issue, apply all three criteria: meaningful reversal cost or impact, real alternatives with materially different consequences, and rationale future maintainers need. For each such decision, obtain positions from at least two distinct native subagents with relevant stakeholder perspectives or independent analyses; the coordinator is not one of the two. Provide the goal, constraints, alternatives, and available evidence, then compare their independent positions and dissent and own the choice. Before marking a decision accepted or final, including in a draft ADR, wait for both actual responses and compare each counted stakeholder and attributed position with them; starting agents is not receiving their positions. Do not infer additional participants or consensus from your synthesis. Record the context, alternatives, choice and evidence, consequences, verification obligations, and material dissent or risks in an ADR even when the task ends at design; write it before any dependent implementation. Follow the project's ADR convention or use `docs/adr/YYYY-MM-DD-<slug>.md`. Reuse an applicable decision and honor explicit requests to document ordinary decisions without imposing a panel on them. If two independent subagents or the ADR cannot be obtained, leave dependent work blocked and continue independent work; do not represent the decision as complete.

When original stakeholder responses need to be recoverable, determine before delegation whether the host can provide reopenable events when possible, then verify the actual event can be reopened. If this is unavailable or uncertain and the user and host permit writing and retaining evidence files at the chosen paths, you may give each stakeholder a distinct new permitted path outside the product target in its initial brief and ask it to write its complete response there and return the same body through the host. Compare each file with its complete actual return and separately inspect the host delegation and attribution before calling it the original. Respect read-only and content restrictions, existing files and links; a path check is not race-free isolation. A file, digest or summary does not authenticate the author or prove independence. If the original, comparison or provenance cannot be recovered, label summaries and report the affected assessment as unverified; do not invent evidence or launch duplicate stakeholders merely to retain a response.

If the user's boundary or the host prevents obtaining the required positions or recording the ADR, continue permitted investigation and report evidence, alternatives, and any clearly provisional recommendation. Identify the missing positions or record; a response is not a recorded ADR. Do not present the formal decision as complete or proceed with dependent work. An investigation or options-only request can reach its requested stopping point, but a request for the formal decision or ADR remains incomplete.

Create durable design records only when requested or needed for the work. Follow existing project conventions and record only actual sources and verification.
