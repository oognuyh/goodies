# Risk and approval policy

Choose risk per authorized change from impact, reversibility, uncertainty, and affected users, and explain the choice. Reassess when new evidence changes the risk. When reclassifying, revisit [Voyage's core-selection conditions](../../SKILL.md) before the next relevant verification; a prior selection for a different risk may no longer apply. File count or a small diff does not establish low impact.

When a change writes durable data or changes its format, loading, validation, or ownership, assess whether failure, restart, rollback, or concurrency could lose data or make existing records unavailable. A credible risk of material data harm is Critical even when the change appears additive; investigate uncertainty instead of assuming safety. In particular, if rolling back to the previous release would make newly written records unreadable and no verified lossless recovery path exists, classify the change as Critical and check that risk. Check the relevant recovery and compatibility boundaries at the assessed risk level.

| Level | Typical fit | Review and verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light | Localized, low-impact, easily reversible change | Focused self-review and a relevant behavior or artifact check. |
| Standard | Ordinary feature or behavior-changing fix | Independent separate-context review of requirements and implementation standards, plus relevant execution and regression or integration checks. |
| Critical | Material harm risk, including access control, payments, destructive migrations, or durable data integrity | Standard requirements plus review and execution checks for the specific security, boundary, concurrency, recovery, or other material risks. |

Light may use self-review. For Standard and Critical, supply the independent reviewer with the original requirements, actual current target or diff, scope, observed current-target execution results, and established implementer/reviewer identities. The reviewer assesses independently and may run checks for a concrete failure hypothesis or unresolved uncertainty; an unchanged check does not need to be repeated merely to duplicate an observed result. If identity, separation, or required input is unknown, do not invent it or count the review as independently verified. Major/critical findings and failed required acceptance criteria block completion. An owner may accept a genuinely minor finding within delegated authority with rationale, impact, and residual risk visible; its failed assessment must not be rewritten as pass.

The user's delegated scope and the host's actual permissions determine authorization. A Critical label by itself does not require a new human approval when the user has already authorized the work and the host permits it. Conversely, verification does not authorize merging, deploying, publishing, external communication, or other actions outside that scope. If the requested next action actually requires approval or permission that is absent, mark that action `approval_required`, do not perform it, and continue independent authorized work. Do not request approval simply because a retry failed. A machine gate is not the host's authority and cannot authorize an action.
