# State, evidence, and decisions

The coordinator manages the Voyage goal in its identified versioned local record when permitted and available, or in the session with an explicit recovery limitation otherwise. Its lifecycle status is `active`, `blocked`, or `completed`; use the store's goal ID and revision for changes, and do not silently overwrite conflicts or recover an unidentified record. Stored statements and evidence references are unauthenticated. Completion requires the store's structural checks and the coordinator's inspection of the actual current evidence against the original request and applicable steering. Reopen a completed or blocked goal as `active` only after reassessing what remains. A record does not schedule a turn or authorize publication.

The following are finer work-result dispositions. Report their meaning and actual supporting evidence; preserve available host state and task records.

| Disposition | Condition and next action |
| --- | --- |
| `draft` | Intent, scope, implementation, or required evidence is incomplete. Continue the applicable playbook. |
| `in_review` | Review is underway for an identified current target and requirements. |
| `changes_requested` | A supported blocking review finding or required acceptance failure needs a fix. Diagnose it, fix within scope, then repeat affected checks and review. |
| `verification_failed` | A required execution check or acceptance condition failed. Diagnose, fix within scope, and repeat affected checks and review. A missing or unavailable check is unverified, not a failure or pass. |
| `verified` | Every applicable required acceptance criterion has current evidence, required checks pass, and required review has no unresolved blocking finding. The test's exit status alone does not establish an outcome. |
| `approval_required` | A specific next action requires authorization that the user or host has not granted. Continue independent authorized work; do not convert a verification result into permission. |
| `ready_to_ship` | The current target is verified and all applicable authorization for the requested shipping action is established. It is not evidence that shipping occurred. |

`verified` and `ready_to_ship` are distinct. A task that only requests a design, diagnosis, or review ends at its requested artifact and does not imply a passing product or a shipping state. A successful diagnosis may prove that the product fails. Do not invent a required gate to force an unrelated task through implementation, review, or deployment.

Connect each material criterion to actual execution or inspection evidence and its target, conditions, and result. Preserve the distinction between a passing command and proof that a particular outcome holds. Record failures, not-run checks, and blocked checks separately. A required check with missing, stale, mismatched, or insufficient evidence remains unverified. Keep requirements review and standards review separate from execution results. A verified conclusion for Standard or Critical work needs a separate-context review with no unresolved blocking findings and all required acceptance criteria and execution checks passing for the same current target. A supported minor finding may be accepted within delegated authority with its original axis result, rationale, and residual risk visible; a required criterion or major/critical finding cannot be waived as minor.

When the target, requirements, relevant environment, or authority changes, invalidate affected evidence, review, and approval; re-evaluate the current target before declaring completion. Target identity must consider relevant tracked changes, untracked inputs, and any other inputs needed by the check. A commit or clean CI run alone does not validate a different dirty workspace. A target fingerprint and matching before/after observations cannot prove that all dependencies were known or that an intermediate change never occurred. State those limitations instead of claiming complete isolation. If a bundled deterministic gate is used, retain its result and resolve a rejection or unknown input rather than overriding it by assertion; such a gate only evaluates the supplied evidence and does not enforce native host actions.

When the local gate finds its supplied records structurally eligible but cannot authenticate review, execution, CI, or approval provenance, the coordinator may inspect the actual host tool results and source records for the current target, verify that they satisfy the real acceptance criteria and required independence or authorization, and retain the basis for that conclusion. The gate's `candidate_state` is a conclusion from supplied records, not the authenticated `verified` or `ready_to_ship` disposition. The coordinator then decides Voyage goal completion. Do not change or ignore the gate's result, represent its structural eligibility as an authenticated pass, or use this separate assessment to waive a missing, unrun, failed, stale, or otherwise insufficient mandatory condition detected by the gate. Task or Voyage goal completion and authorization to ship remain separate.

The gate checks consistency with the intent and target the caller supplied; it cannot establish that they include every original requirement or protected file. Before a completion claim, compare the actual user request and applicable steering with the derived intent, units, preservation checks, current target, and evidence. A new fingerprint for a narrowed intent is not evidence that the user authorized the narrowing. If an original requirement or protected state is unaccounted for, assess it separately and do not call the requested outcome complete.

Classify review feedback and verification failures before retrying. An implementation defect can return to an authorized fix; an invalid test or missing dependency needs its own correction or evidence. If the findings reveal inconsistent requirements, an incorrect contract, an insufficient design, or an inappropriate existing decision, revisit the intent and affected design instead of repeatedly patching the same implementation. Compare the corrected alternatives and reconsider existing ADR applicability; if the new decision meets the ADR criteria below, obtain both independent positions and record it before dependent changes. Do not silently change the user's requested outcome or expand the scope while revisiting design. If essential intent or authority cannot be resolved, continue independent work and report the affected work as blocked or unverified.

## ADR-worthy decisions

A decision is ADR-worthy when all three hold: reversal has meaningful cost or impact; real alternatives have materially different consequences; and future maintainers need the rationale. Reuse an applicable decision. Otherwise obtain positions from at least two distinct native subagents, each with a relevant stakeholder perspective, the decision, alternatives, constraints, and available evidence. The coordinator is not one of the two. Compare their evidence and dissent, decide as coordinator, and record an ADR before dependent implementation, including when the task stops at design. Follow the project's ADR convention or use `docs/adr/YYYY-MM-DD-<slug>.md`.

Record the question, context, alternatives, choice, evidence, consequences, verification obligations, and material dissent. Agent input is not human approval. If the two separate positions or ADR cannot be obtained, block dependent work and continue unrelated authorized work. Routine choices do not require a council or ADR.
